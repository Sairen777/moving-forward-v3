# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technically-curious readers and working developers, plus the author writing for themselves in public. They arrive from a link (a shared post, a feed, a search) in a spare moment, on a laptop or phone, expecting to read rather than to act. The job is simple: read one considered piece on building software and the craft of doing it well, and feel it was worth the time. For the author, the site is a durable, public place to think in writing and keep a record of work and lessons.

## Product Purpose

"moving forward" is a personal field journal: essays and notes on building software, reading code you did not write, and the discipline of finishing what you start. It exists so the author has a calm place to think out loud, and so readers have something unhurried and specific to read instead of a content feed. Success looks like a visitor staying to read rather than bouncing, the work reading as hand-made rather than templated, and the author wanting to come back and write the next entry.

## Brand Personality

Calm, considered, quiet. The voice is first-person, plain, and specific: it describes what was actually done and what it cost, never reaching for hype or aphorism. The interface should evoke room to think, an unhurried pace, and the sense that one person made every decision on purpose. Confidence shown through restraint, not volume.

## Anti-references

- Busy, heavy SaaS landing pages: hero-metric templates, dense card grids, stacked calls to action.
- Generic dev-blog templates (the Medium / Dev.to sameness): default sans, boxed cards, sidebar clutter.
- Loud, attention-grabbing marketing motion: scroll-jacking, parallax spectacle, an entrance animation on every element.
- Corporate or enterprise polish: navy-and-gradient sterility, stock gloss, anything that reads as a committee made it.
- Anything that reads as AI-generated or templated. The goal is unmistakably hand-built.

## Design Principles

1. Serenity over stimulation. The page should feel like space to breathe. Spend weight, color, and motion sparingly; the default is quiet.
2. The hand shows. Bespoke details (the generated ASCII-wheat field, the monospace voice) prove a person made this, and are worth more than any template could buy.
3. Words first. Typography and reading comfort outrank decoration. The writing is the subject; everything else gets out of its way.
4. Motion as weather, not fireworks. Movement is ambient and continuous, like wind over a field. It never demands attention, blocks reading, or gates content behind a reveal.
5. Restraint is the identity. Fewer elements, more space. The absence of clutter is the brand, so adding something has to beat leaving it out.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target set by the author. Baseline still held in practice: honor prefers-reduced-motion (the animated field already falls back to a static frame), keep body and heading text at legible contrast on the pale background, and use semantic, keyboard-navigable HTML. Revisit with a concrete WCAG AA pass if the audience or context ever calls for it.
